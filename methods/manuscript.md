# Seed-anchored co-dependency mapping of the DOT1L–H3K79 axis places MENIN–MLL, not the DotCom paralogues, at the centre of the DOT1L dependency neighbourhood

## Abstract

DOT1L is the sole H3K79 methyltransferase and a long-standing therapeutic target in KMT2A-rearranged (MLL-r) leukaemia, but the clinical performance of catalytic DOT1L inhibition has been modest, motivating a systematic view of which other gene products share DOT1L's dependency structure. We assembled a focused co-dependency network from seven CRISPR gene-effect correlation profiles (DOT1L, MLLT10/AF10, MLLT6/AF17, MLLT1/ENL, MLLT3/AF9, KMT2A, MEN1; DepMap Public 26Q1+Score, Chronos) and integrated it with STRING protein-association topology. Requiring positive correlation with at least two of seven seeds, or r ≥ 0.35 with one, reduced 454 candidate genes to a 127-gene network resolving into thirteen modules. Calibrating that selection against a Poisson-binomial null, with the eligible gene universe estimated by capture–recapture from the lists themselves and validated against two internally unrelated seeds, shows the two-seed threshold carries an 85% false-discovery rate; we therefore tier every gene and define a 66-gene high-confidence set at recurrence ≥ 3 (FDR 15%) or ≥ 4 (1.5%). Under that filter the chromatin modules retain 71% of their members and the metabolic modules 23%, confirming on statistical grounds that the latter reflect lineage covariance. Three results stand out. First, the strongest relationships in the entire network are internal to the MENIN–MLL axis (KMT2A–MEN1 r = 0.58; DOT1L–MEN1 r = 0.57; DOT1L–KMT2A r = 0.52), providing genome-scale genetic support for the pharmacological complementarity already reported for menin and DOT1L inhibitors. Second, the DOT1L-containing complex enters the network asymmetrically: AF10 (r = 0.45) and ENL (r = 0.38) track with DOT1L while AF17 (r = 0.17) and AF9 (no reported correlation) do not. Third, the most reproducible single partner across the axis is *KAT6A*, recurring in six of seven seed profiles within an eleven-gene MOZ/MORF–HBO1 acetyltransferase module. Conversely, the H2B-ubiquitination ligases *RNF20* and *RNF40*, which are biochemically required to stimulate DOT1L, are absent from all seven profiles, while the SAGA H2B-deubiquitinase module is present in full. We position these results against the existing genome-wide co-essentiality literature, state explicitly what this focused analysis adds and what it does not, and identify the specific additional data needed to separate MLL-r-specific structure from pan-cancer lineage covariance.

## Introduction

H3K79 methylation by DOT1L was linked to leukaemogenesis two decades ago, when hDOT1L was shown to be recruited by MLL fusion partners and to drive aberrant target-gene expression ([Okada et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2005.02.020)). The dependency of MLL-rearranged leukaemia on this activity was subsequently established genetically ([Bernt et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ccr.2011.06.010)) and exploited pharmacologically with the selective inhibitor EPZ004777 ([Daigle et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ccr.2011.06.009)). DOT1L does not act alone: it assembles with AF10, AF17, ENL and AF9 in the DotCom complex ([Mohan et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1101/gad.1898410)), and the AF10 interaction in particular is required for processive conversion of H3K79me1 to me2/me3 ([Deshpande et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ccell.2014.10.009); [Song et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1904672116)).

Despite a clean mechanistic rationale, the clinical translation has been disappointing. The DOT1L inhibitor pinometostat reduced H3K79 methylation in patients but produced only modest clinical activity as a single agent ([Stein et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1182/blood-2017-12-818948)), and resistance arises readily in laboratory models ([Campbell et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1158/1535-7163.MCT-16-0693); [Schneider et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40164-023-00445-8)). Meanwhile, inhibition of the menin–MLL interaction has advanced further, from preclinical eradication of disease in MLL-r and NPM1-mutant models ([Krivtsov et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ccell.2019.11.001)) to registrational clinical data for revumenib ([Issa et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.24.00826)). Combination of the two mechanisms has been proposed on the basis of complementary and synergistic activity in MLL-r models ([Dafflon et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/leu.2016.327); [Adriaanse et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms25116020)).

Genome-scale loss-of-function screening offers an orthogonal way to ask which gene products share a functional requirement. The Cancer Dependency Map provides gene-effect estimates across more than a thousand cancer cell lines ([Tsherniak et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2017.06.010)), corrected for copy-number artefacts ([Meyers et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng.3984)) and, in current releases, modelled with Chronos ([Dempster et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-021-02540-7)). The premise of co-essentiality analysis is that genes whose knockout fitness profiles correlate across cell lines tend to act in the same complex or pathway — a premise validated for protein-complex membership ([Pan et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cels.2018.04.011)) and extended into genome-wide module atlases ([Kim et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.26508/lsa.201800278); [Boyle et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.15252/msb.20188594); [Wainberg et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-021-00840-z)).

Here we apply that logic in a deliberately narrow, seed-anchored form. Rather than partitioning the genome, we ask which genes recur across the co-dependency neighbourhoods of seven genes that are physically or functionally tied to H3K79 methylation, and what structure those recurrent genes form.

## Methods

**Dependency data.** Seven per-gene co-dependency profiles were obtained from the DepMap portal, dataset `CRISPR (DepMap Public 26Q1+Score, Chronos)`, for DOT1L, MLLT10, MLLT6, MLLT1, MLLT3, KMT2A and MEN1. Each profile lists the genes most strongly correlated, positively and negatively, with the seed's Chronos gene-effect vector across cell lines. The "+Score" dataset integrates Broad and Sanger screens ([Pacini et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-21898-7)), so correlation magnitudes are not numerically interchangeable with a Broad-only release. Profile lengths after merging were 174 (DOT1L), 144 (MLLT1), 129 (MLLT3), 128 (MLLT10), 117 (MEN1), 103 (MLLT6) and 82 (KMT2A) genes, for 877 seed–gene records. Correlations are pan-cancer and are not restricted to KMT2A-rearranged lines.

**Node selection.** Merging the profiles yielded 454 genes with at least one positive correlation to a seed. Recurrence was distributed steeply: 332 genes appeared in exactly one profile, 56 in two, 31 in three, 24 in four, 10 in five and one in six. A gene was admitted to the network if it was positively correlated with at least two of the seven seeds, or reached r ≥ 0.35 with a single seed, giving 127 nodes. The purpose of this filter is reproducibility across the axis rather than statistical significance, which the portal exports do not report. The 327 excluded single-seed genes are retained in the accompanying node table.

**Topology and modules.** Protein-association edges among the 127 nodes were retrieved from STRING v12 ([Szklarczyk et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkac1000)) at combined score ≥ 0.4, giving 505 edges. One of 127 symbols was resolved to a synonym (CDIN1 → C15orf41, the same gene); all others matched exactly. Modules were detected by weighted label propagation ([Raghavan et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.76.036106)) with a fixed random seed. A densely connected 41-gene component enriched only for the generic term "nucleoplasm" was re-resolved hierarchically at score thresholds of 0.7 and then 0.9, which separated the H3K79 writer arm, MENIN–MLL–LEDGF, PRC2, MYB–p300 and ncBAF. Modules were named by their lowest-FDR STRING functional enrichment term, not by author judgement.

**Statistical calibration.** Because the portal exports carry no p-values and are truncated, four calibration analyses were run. (i) *Censoring.* The smallest reported positive correlation per seed defines the truncation threshold, so absence from a list is an interval-censored observation rather than a null result. (ii) *Effective gene universe.* The seven lists were treated as repeated captures and the number of genes eligible to appear in any list was estimated by the bias-corrected Chao richness estimator ([Chao 1987](https://doi.org/10.2307/2531532); [Colwell and Coddington 1994](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.1994.0091)). (iii) *Recurrence null.* Under independent sampling of each list from a universe of size *N*, the recurrence count of a gene is Poisson-binomial; the exact distribution was convolved to give each gene a p-value, P(R ≥ r) under the null, over a test set comprising the full estimated universe. These were converted to q-values by the Benjamini–Hochberg step-up procedure ([Benjamini and Hochberg 1995](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1995.tb02031.x)); because the p-values are heavily tied, all genes at a given recurrence level receive the common q-value of their tie group. Equivalently and to within 1.1 × 10⁻⁴, this q-value is the ratio of the expected to the observed number of genes reaching that recurrence level, so the filter's false-discovery rate and the per-gene q-value are the same quantity. Pairwise list overlaps were tested hypergeometrically with the same correction. Both the discreteness of the p-values and the positive dependence among seeds make this correction conservative rather than exact. (iv) *Module stability.* Label propagation was repeated with 400 random orderings and the pairwise co-assignment frequency recorded, following consensus-clustering practice ([Lancichinetti and Fortunato 2012](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep00336)).

**Outputs.** The final network comprises 127 nodes and 867 edges (505 STRING associations, 362 seed-to-gene co-dependency edges), distributed as GraphML with node attributes, a per-gene confidence tier and a module-stability score.

## Results

### Network composition

The 127-node network resolved into thirteen named modules plus 42 nodes that fell in STRING components of fewer than two genes. Module sizes were: mitochondrial OXPHOS (17), SAGA (12), MOZ/MORF–HBO1 acetyltransferase (11), Mediator (10), H3K79 writer/DotCom (6), nucleotide biosynthesis (6), heme and sugar metabolism (4), haematopoietic transcription factors (4), NAD synthesis (4), PRC2 (3), MENIN–MLL–LEDGF (3), MYB–p300 (3) and ncBAF (2).

### Calibration of the recurrence filter

The recurrence criterion is the load-bearing step of the analysis, and it can be calibrated against a null even without the gene-effect matrix. Treating the seven lists as repeated captures of an unknown pool, 625 distinct genes were observed, of which 489 appeared exactly once and 67 exactly twice, giving a bias-corrected Chao estimate of approximately 2,380 genes eligible to appear in any list. This is far smaller than the roughly 18,000-gene screening library, because a gene needs appreciable variance in its gene-effect profile before it can correlate strongly with anything.

That distinction matters, because the chance of spurious recurrence scales inversely with the universe size. Under a Poisson-binomial null, the expected number of genes reaching recurrence ≥ 2 by chance is 17.8 if the universe is 18,000 but 115.5 if it is 2,380, against 136 observed. The false-discovery rate of the ≥ 2 threshold is therefore between 13% and 85% depending on an assumption the data must settle (Figure 2a).

The data do settle it. MLLT3 and MLLT6 function as internal negative controls: neither shares appreciable dependency with DOT1L, so their list overlaps estimate chance co-occurrence directly. Every pair involving MLLT3 or MLLT6 overlapped at or below the independence expectation computed at N = 2,380 (observed/expected 0.13–0.86), whereas every pair among the five related seeds was enriched 3.4- to 8.7-fold (all q < 10⁻⁵, hypergeometric with Benjamini–Hochberg correction). The independence null at the Chao universe size is therefore approximately calibrated, or mildly conservative, for unrelated gene pairs.

Accepting that calibration, the ≥ 2 threshold used in our initial network carries a Benjamini–Hochberg q of 0.849 and is not defensible. Recurrence ≥ 3 gives q = 0.147, ≥ 4 gives q = 0.015, ≥ 5 gives q = 0.0016 and the single gene at ≥ 6 gives q = 3.0 × 10⁻⁴. We therefore retain the 127-node network as the permissive view but define a high-confidence subset of 66 genes at recurrence ≥ 3, and annotate every gene with a tier: A (recurrence ≥ 4, 35 genes), B (recurrence = 3, 31 genes), C (everything else, 388 genes).

The effect of this filter is strongly non-uniform across modules (Figure 2b). The chromatin modules survive nearly intact: SAGA retains 11 of 12 members, MENIN–MLL–LEDGF 3 of 3, PRC2 3 of 3, MOZ/MORF–HBO1 7 of 11 and Mediator 5 of 10. The metabolic modules largely do not: mitochondrial OXPHOS falls from 17 genes to 3, nucleotide biosynthesis from 6 to 1 and NAD synthesis from 4 to 0. The suspicion stated informally in our first pass — that the metabolic blocks reflect lineage and proliferation covariance rather than H3K79 biology — is thus confirmed on statistical rather than rhetorical grounds.

Two further calibrations bear on interpretation. First, module assignment is robust to the stochastic element of label propagation: across 400 random orderings, mean within-module co-assignment was 1.00 for SAGA, Mediator, PRC2, MENIN–MLL–LEDGF and ncBAF, 0.99 for MOZ/MORF–HBO1 and mitochondrial OXPHOS, and 0.87 for the H3K79 writer module, the least stable because its members bridge to the MENIN–MLL and SAGA blocks. Second, nominal significance of the individual correlations is not a useful discriminator. The smallest correlation appearing in any export is 0.142; for a panel of 800 or more cell lines that corresponds to p < 10⁻⁴, and the Bonferroni threshold for 18,000 tests falls at r ≈ 0.135–0.165 over the same range. The portal's truncation therefore sits at approximately the genome-wide significance boundary already, so essentially every listed correlation is nominally significant and the discrimination must come from effect size and recurrence instead.

### The MENIN–MLL axis dominates the network

The strongest pairwise relationships in the entire analysis are internal to the MENIN–MLL–DOT1L triad: KMT2A–MEN1 r = 0.58, DOT1L–MEN1 r = 0.57 and DOT1L–KMT2A r = 0.52. *PSIP1*, encoding LEDGF, follows at r = 0.53, completing a three-gene module whose members are exactly the components of the menin–LEDGF–MLL targeting module characterised biochemically ([Yokoyama and Cleary 2008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ccr.2008.05.003)) and shown to be required for MLL-r leukaemogenesis in vivo ([El Ashkar et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1182/blood-2017-05-786962)).

Because all three seeds intercorrelate, each pairwise value could in principle be induced by the third. Partial correlations show it is not: r(DOT1L, MEN1) falls from 0.570 to 0.383 when KMT2A is controlled for, r(KMT2A, MEN1) from 0.583 to 0.407 controlling for DOT1L, and r(DOT1L, KMT2A) from 0.524 to 0.287 controlling for MEN1. The triad is a genuine three-way relationship rather than a chain through any single member. The same test applied to *PSIP1* is informative: its correlation with MEN1 is 0.53 against a transitivity-implied value of 0.21 given its link to DOT1L, so LEDGF is tied to menin directly rather than through the writer.

The practical reading is that the cell lines requiring DOT1L are largely the same population requiring MENIN and KMT2A itself. This is a genetic, genome-scale restatement of what has been observed pharmacologically — that menin and DOT1L inhibitors have complementary activity in MLL-r models ([Dafflon et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/leu.2016.327)) and synergise in KMT2A-r ALL ([Adriaanse et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms25116020)). It is not an independent discovery of that relationship, but it is independent evidence for it, derived from perturbation data rather than from drug combinations.

### The writer complex enters through AF10 and ENL only

DotCom paralogues behave asymmetrically. DOT1L correlates with MLLT10/AF10 at r = 0.45 and MLLT1/ENL at r = 0.38, but only 0.17 with MLLT6/AF17, and MLLT3/AF9 reports no correlation with DOT1L at all. Neither MLLT6 nor MLLT3 contributed any node of its own to the network.

For AF10 this is the expected result: the AF10–DOT1L coiled-coil interaction is required for processive H3K79 di- and tri-methylation ([Deshpande et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ccell.2014.10.009); [Song et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1904672116)). For ENL, the co-dependency is consistent with its established standalone requirement in AML, where its YEATS domain couples histone acetylation to oncogenic transcription ([Erb et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature21688); [Wan et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature21687)). AF9 is also capable of recruiting DOT1L, and the degree of recruitment sets H3K79 methylation levels at MLL-AF9 target genes ([Kuntimaddi et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2015.04.004)) — so its absence here is best read as fitness-level redundancy with ENL across a pan-cancer panel, not as evidence against a physical or regulatory relationship. An important technical caveat applies to all negative results of this kind. A correlation requires variance in both gene-effect profiles, so a gene that is dispensable in nearly every line has no signal to correlate, irrespective of its biology.

### KAT6A is the most reproducible partner outside the MLL axis

*KAT6A* recurs in six of seven seed profiles, more than any other gene in the analysis, with a maximum correlation of 0.41. It is the only gene at that recurrence level (q = 3.0 × 10⁻⁴), and its association is not merely transitive: its correlations with KMT2A (0.36) and MLLT1 (0.36) exceed the values of 0.21 and 0.15 implied by transitivity through DOT1L. It sits in an eleven-gene module (STRING enrichment "histone acetyltransferase complex", FDR 6 × 10⁻²³) comprising *BRPF1* (0.43), *JADE2* (0.42), *KAT6A* (0.41), *MEAF6* (0.33), *ING5* (0.31), *KAT6B*, *MORF4L1*, *JADE1*, *KAT7*, *MRGBP* and *MSL3*: the MOZ/MORF and HBO1 acetyltransferase complexes together with their shared scaffolding subunits.

This nomination requires immediate qualification. KAT6A is a tractable target, and the KAT6A/B inhibitor WM-1119 arrests tumour growth and induces senescence in other contexts ([Baell et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0387-5)). However, a direct test reported that WM-1119 is effective against KAT6A-rearranged AML but *not* against KMT2A-rearranged AML ([Sheridan et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13045-024-01610-0)). A shared genetic dependency profile therefore does not imply that catalytic inhibition of KAT6A will be efficacious in MLL-r disease; the co-dependency may reflect a scaffolding or complex-integrity requirement that a catalytic-site inhibitor does not reproduce. This is the clearest instance in our results where correlation structure and pharmacology diverge.

### SAGA, Mediator and PRC2 appear as intact complexes

SAGA is recovered essentially complete (12 members, FDR 3 × 10⁻³¹), led by *SGF29* (0.38), *TADA2B* (0.38), *SUPT20H* (0.37), *TAF5L* (0.36) and *TADA1* (0.36), and including the H2B-deubiquitinase sub-module *USP22*, *ATXN7* and *ATXN7L3*. Mediator contributes ten subunits led by *MED13* (0.36), and PRC2 contributes *EZH2* (0.42), *EED* (0.38) and *SUZ12* (0.35). The positive sign of the PRC2 correlation indicates that DOT1L-dependent lines tend also to be EZH2-dependent, consistent with PRC2 supporting aberrant self-renewal in MLL-AF9 models ([Shi et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1038/onc.2012.110)); it reads as a parallel requirement rather than an escape route.

### A biochemically required cofactor arm is absent

Neither *RNF20* nor *RNF40*, nor the E2 enzymes *UBE2A* and *UBE2B*, appears in any of the seven profiles. This is notable because H2B lysine-120 ubiquitination directly stimulates DOT1L, a relationship resolved structurally ([Valencia-Sánchez et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2019.03.029); [Zhang et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2019.05.013); reviewed in [Worden and Wolberger 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sbi.2019.05.009)), and because RNF20 has been reported to be required for MLL-rearranged leukaemia ([Wang et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1301045110)). The co-dependency data neither confirm nor refute that requirement: absence from a truncated top-ranked list means the correlation did not reach approximately the hundredth rank for any seed, which is censoring rather than a null test. The asymmetry remains interesting, in that the deubiquitinase module scores while the ligase module does not, but it should be treated as a hypothesis about differential fitness coupling, not as a contradiction of the biochemistry.

Also absent from all seven profiles are the PAF1 complex (*PAF1*, *CTR9*, *LEO1*, *CDC73*, *RTF1*), SAM-cycle genes (*MAT2A*, *MAT2B*, *MTAP*), super-elongation-complex members beyond *AFF1* (*AFF4*, *ELL*, *ELL2*, *CDK9*), elongation and FACT subunits (*SUPT5H*, *SUPT6H*, *SSRP1*, *SUPT16H*), and the canonical fusion-target output genes *HOXA9*, *MEIS1* and *PBX3*. The last of these is expected, since transcriptional targets of the fusion need not themselves be fitness genes whose knockout profiles correlate with the writer.

### Anti-correlated genes

Negative correlations are sparse: 174 genes carry at least one negative value, but only 12 recur across two or more seeds. The numerically largest negative values are single-seed observations in poorly characterised genes (*KRBOX4* −0.44, *PLCXD1* −0.43, *NLRC3* −0.42) and are most plausibly noise. Among reproducible negatives, a coherent integrin–talin adhesion set appears: *TLN1* (3 seeds, mean r = −0.23), *USP19* (−0.22), *ILK* (−0.21), *FERMT2* (−0.19) and *TNS2* (−0.17). We interpret this as a lineage contrast, since DOT1L-dependent lines are predominantly haematopoietic and non-adherent, rather than as a synthetic-lethal relationship.

## Discussion

### Relation to prior co-essentiality work

The analytical premise used here is not new, and it is important to be precise about what is and is not original. That correlated CRISPR fitness profiles recover protein complexes was established by [Pan et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cels.2018.04.011), who used co-essentiality to infer complex membership and structure directly. [Boyle et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.15252/msb.20188594) and [Kim et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.26508/lsa.201800278) built genome-scale co-functional interaction networks from knockout fitness screens. Most relevant, [Wainberg et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-021-00840-z) produced a genome-wide atlas of co-essential modules with bias-corrected correlations and calibrated false-discovery control, explicitly designed to assign function to uncharacterised genes. A DOT1L-containing module almost certainly exists within that atlas.

A fourth piece of prior work is a tool rather than an atlas. shinyDepMap ([Shimada et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.57116)) clusters genes by dependency-profile similarity at multiple granularities and exposes efficacy and selectivity scores through an interactive browser, specifically to identify targetable genes and their functional connections. A user of that tool could retrieve DOT1L's cluster in a few clicks.

**What the present analysis does that those do not.** First, it is seed-anchored rather than global. The atlases partition the genome and then ask what each partition contains. Here the question is inverted: given seven genes known to be physically or functionally tied to H3K79 methylation, which partners recur across their neighbourhoods? The recurrence criterion (positive correlation with ≥ 2 of 7 seeds) is a reproducibility filter that has no counterpart in a global clustering, and it is what demotes 327 of 454 candidate genes. Second, it reports informative negatives within a defined complex. A global atlas reports what clusters; it does not draw attention to the fact that two of four DotCom paralogues fail to cluster with DOT1L, because AF9 and AF17 are simply placed elsewhere or nowhere. The paralogue asymmetry is only visible if the paralogues are interrogated deliberately as a set. Third, it uses a substantially more recent data release. The prior studies were built on 2018–2021 releases with roughly 400–500 cell lines and, in most cases, CERES gene effects; the present profiles are 26Q1 Chronos scores integrating Broad and Sanger screens, approximately double the cell-line panel. Fourth, module identity is assigned by independent protein-association evidence — STRING topology and its enrichment — rather than by the dependency correlations alone, so the module labels are not circular with respect to the clustering input.

**What it does not do better.** The statistical rigour remains lower than that of the published atlases, though the gap is narrower than it first appears. Wainberg and colleagues correct for systematic bias in correlation estimates by removing dominant principal components before computing correlations, and that correction is simply unavailable here: it requires the gene-effect matrix, not rank lists. We can calibrate the *selection* step, as above, but not the correlations themselves, so any inflation shared across the axis from proliferation rate, screen quality or lineage structure remains in every value we report. This is the single most consequential methodological deficit, and it is not closable with the data in hand. Its scope is also a few hundred genes rather than the genome. It inherits a hard truncation at approximately the hundredth-ranked partner per seed, so every negative statement is censored rather than tested. And its module assignments inherit STRING's literature bias, which is why 42 of 127 nodes, disproportionately poorly studied genes, could not be placed. This is a focused, hypothesis-generating re-analysis on current data, not a methodological advance over the atlas literature.

### Interpretation and clinical bearing

The strongest signal, the tightness of the DOT1L–MENIN–KMT2A triad, is also the most actionable and the most consistent with existing pharmacology. If the lines requiring DOT1L largely coincide with those requiring MENIN, the two agents address substantially overlapping populations, which argues for combination rather than sequence and also predicts shared resistance liabilities. Given that single-agent pinometostat produced target engagement but limited clinical benefit ([Stein et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1182/blood-2017-12-818948)) while menin inhibition has reached registrational data ([Issa et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.24.00826)), the combination rationale already supported in models ([Dafflon et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/leu.2016.327); [Adriaanse et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms25116020)) gains an orthogonal genetic argument here.

The KAT6A result is the most interesting nomination and simultaneously the one most in need of caution, because the one direct pharmacological test available reports a negative outcome in exactly the genetic context of interest ([Sheridan et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13045-024-01610-0)). The useful next experiment is therefore a degradation or complex-disruption approach rather than another catalytic inhibitor, since the co-dependency may report a requirement for complex integrity that catalytic blockade does not reproduce.

### Principal limitation

The correlations analysed here are pan-cancer. The decisive therapeutic contrast, co-dependency structure *within* KMT2A-rearranged lines versus everything else, is not modelled. Four of the thirteen modules (mitochondrial OXPHOS, nucleotide biosynthesis, NAD synthesis, heme and sugar metabolism, 31 nodes in total) are most economically explained as lineage and proliferation covariance shared among haematopoietic lines rather than as H3K79-specific biology. The recurrence calibration supports this reading: those modules lose 24 of their 31 members at a defensible threshold (retaining 23%), while the chromatin modules retain 32 of 45 (71%). They are reported here as structure rather than as mechanism. Resolving this requires the full `CRISPRGeneEffect` matrix together with cell-line and fusion annotation, which would permit MLL-r versus non-MLL-r differential dependency with effect sizes and formal FDR, and recomputation of co-dependency restricted to KMT2A-rearranged lines.

## Conclusion

A reproducibility-filtered, seed-anchored co-dependency map of the H3K79 axis places the MENIN–MLL–LEDGF module, not the DotCom paralogues, closest to DOT1L; shows that only AF10 and ENL among the writer complex's partners share DOT1L's dependency structure; and nominates the MOZ/MORF–HBO1 acetyltransferase module, led by the exceptionally recurrent *KAT6A*, as the most reproducible associated vulnerability. Existing pharmacological data caution against interpreting that nomination as catalytic tractability in MLL-r disease. Calibrating the selection step against a null drawn from the data shows that a two-seed recurrence criterion is mostly chance, that a three-seed criterion is defensible, and that the metabolic modules which dominate the permissive network do not survive it, whereas the chromatin modules largely do. These are hypotheses generated from pan-cancer correlation structure, with the correlations themselves uncorrected for the systematic covariance that a gene-effect matrix would allow us to remove, and they require the KMT2A-r-restricted analysis before they can be treated as MLL-r-specific.
